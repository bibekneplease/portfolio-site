EFTPOS & POS Connectivity Troubleshooting

A methodical first-response workflow for payment terminal connectivity across SIM-based and Ethernet-connected retail environments.

BY BIBEK CHAUDHARY　·　8 MIN READ　·　PRACTICAL GUIDE

Purpose

This guide is a first-line diagnostic sequence for support technicians. Follow the retailer's approved runbook and payment-provider instructions. Do not access, record or share cardholder data, PINs, merchant credentials or transaction details.

1. Establish the scope before touching equipment

  • Ask whether one terminal, one lane, one store or multiple sites are affected.
  • Confirm the exact symptom: no power, no network, slow response, host unavailable, or a transaction-specific decline.
  • Record the time first observed, affected terminal identifiers under approved policy, recent changes and business impact.
  • Check whether the issue is intermittent or continuous and whether other services at the site are working.

2. Check power and physical connections

  • Confirm the terminal and any approved dock or network accessory have power.
  • Inspect the power lead and Ethernet cable for visible damage, loose connections or accidental disconnection.
  • For Ethernet, check link/activity indicators at the terminal or approved network port where available.
  • Do not move cables between ports or change switch configuration without authorisation.

3. SIM / mobile-connected terminals

  • Check the device's displayed mobile signal and whether the provider reports a known service issue.
  • Establish whether the fault follows one terminal or affects several devices in the same area.
  • Note location, signal indication and time of failure; indoor coverage and local carrier incidents can affect service.
  • Do not remove, swap or re-provision SIMs unless the payment provider's procedure explicitly directs it.

4. Ethernet / LAN-connected terminals

  • Confirm the cable is seated and the approved port shows link.
  • Ask the network team to validate the expected VLAN, DHCP scope, gateway, DNS and permitted payment-provider destinations against the site standard.
  • Where authorised, use approved diagnostics to determine whether the device has received the expected network configuration.
  • Do not assign a static IP, change VLANs, disable security controls or open firewall rules as an unapproved workaround.

5. Separate network faults from payment-host faults

A terminal can have local network connectivity while the payment service is unavailable. Compare symptoms across lanes, sites and providers; check the provider's service-status channel and approved escalation route. Avoid repeated test transactions unless the merchant's procedure permits them.

6. Escalate with useful evidence

Provide the service desk or payment provider with the impact, affected scope, start time, terminal/device reference allowed by policy, connection type, visible status/error text, checks completed and any recent change. Keep sensitive payment data out of ticket notes and screenshots.

Quick decision path

  1. No power: follow approved power/device support process.
  2. No Ethernet link: escalate physical port/cabling checks to the site network team.
  3. SIM signal absent: check carrier/provider status and approved device support path.
  4. Network present, host unavailable: investigate provider-side service and permitted connectivity.
  5. Only one transaction declined: use the payment provider's transaction support process; do not diagnose by collecting card data.

Field practice: a good ticket captures scope, symptom, timeline, checks and impact. It does not contain payment credentials or cardholder information.
